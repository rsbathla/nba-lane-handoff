## Verdict

**Most of what X says about preseason roles matches the box score. The posts are most useful where they explain why a preseason start or usage number is misleading. For opening night, that applies most to San Antonio and Oklahoma City. The full notes from Thunder Dan, Adam Stock, Myguyknowsaguy and Besbris, read in your Chrome, are now in the digest.**

What matters for the Oct 20 board:

1. **Spurs (OKC @ SAS).**
   - Mitch Johnson said on 10-08 that he is starting the three guards (Fox, Castle, Harper) with Vassell and Wembanyama (Jared Weiss). The 10-14 box score shows exactly that five starting.
   - Thunder Dan adds that Harris, Keldon Johnson, Kornet and Champagnie were all out that night, and asks "who knows if they really do this." Champagnie's 3-for-3 preseason starts all came in games Fox missed.
   - So neither five has been tested with everyone available. The board's "started 100% of preseason games" line on Champagnie overstates what we know, and Fox (1 game) gets no start line at all. Read the Spurs start lines as open until lineups are confirmed at lock.
2. **Thunder (OKC @ SAS).**
   - Mara started 3 of 4 games and Stirtz 2 of 4, but Holmgren and Jaylin Williams have not played. Holmgren was listed out on 10-13 with a quad contusion (Justin Martinez).
   - Daigneault on 10-14 said the Mara/Stirtz rotation is "fluid night to night" (Clemente Almanza). Thunder Dan (10-12 notes) is blunt that they are "not going to be in the regular season rotation" on a 15-deep team; Myguyknowsaguy thinks Stirtz could make it. Besbris on 10-15 is asking what's going on with Holmgren.
   - The board's start lines on Mara and Stirtz are opportunity left open by absences, not a role. Watch Holmgren on the injury report.
3. **Sixers (PHI @ NYK).**
   - Simons, Bona and Barlow have played no preseason games (hamstring, foot, back). Nurse on 10-13 had "some hope" for Friday's finale (Derek Bodner).
   - LeBron has played once: 18 minutes, one shot, sat the second half. Adam Stock puts that night at a 15.3% usage rate (my box formula gives 16.2%).
   - Adam Stock's bigger flag from the 10-08 game: Embiid looked bad, with 8 turnovers in 24 minutes (the box score shows 28% usage).
   - Edgecombe has started all 3 games at 23 minutes, and Stock sees him as a likely third option.
   - Wade's and Walker's preseason starts came with those players out.
4. **Knicks (PHI @ NYK).**
   - The regular five started all 3 games they played, at 15 to 22 minutes. Thunder Dan flags Towns's assists on 10-12 as a possible hub role.
   - The 10-13 game against Toronto was a full rest night: the five starters plus Clarkson, Drummond and Shamet did not dress (Zulfi Sheikh). That night's reserve numbers say nothing about roles.
5. **Celtics and Pistons (BOS @ DET).**
   - Boston started White, Walsh, George, Tatum and Mitchell Robinson in both of its last two games (Justin Turpin), which matches the box score. Queta (a starter last season) came off the bench both nights. Mazzulla tried Robinson and Queta together late on 10-14 (Myguyknowsaguy, Jay King).
   - Tatum took 19 shots in 26 minutes on 10-10 (Thunder Dan), and his preseason usage is 28.8%.
   - Duren played once (10-14, started, 15 minutes, 24 points, 38% usage) after the contract standoff. Reed's 3 games of center minutes came while Duren was out.

**Preseason usage is a weak signal for stars.** SGA ran at 16.8% usage in preseason against 33.7% last season, and Wembanyama, Brunson and Tatum are near their usual rates. Stars sit second halves and coaches stagger lineups, so a low preseason usage on an established star means nothing. A high number on a bench player is the part worth watching: McCain ran at 29-30% usage in two games with Mitchell playing and 34% on 10-13 without him (Thunder Dan flagged only that last game).

- 2026-10-12 **Andrew Weatherman** (@andreweatherman) · your bookmark [post](https://x.com/andreweatherman/status/2109687324548239520)
  > Little basketball analytics rant.
  >
  > I’ve been seeing more and more people on my timeline use points-per-possession (“PPP”) when talking about actions (and even shooting?). Maybe my timeline has just gotten more college basketball-heavy, but PPP has quietly become a catch-all stat, and it’s getting stretched way past what it measures.
  >
  > PPP is a team efficiency number. It’s great for game- or season-long efficiency, and it’s usually what you’re seeing reported as offensive/defensive rating.
  >
  > When we’re measuring action-level events (picks, drives, isolations, etc.), we want points-per-chance (“PPC”). A chance splits possessions into distinct scoring opportunities, broken up by offensive rebounds and dead balls.
  >
  > Say you run a pindown and the pass gets deflected out of bounds. From the side-out, you run something else and score two. The pindown produced zero points, but PPP credits it with two because you scored on the same possession. PPC gives it zero.
  >
  > If you want to be more precise, you can attribute putback points to the chance that created the miss. Then there’s the concept of direct chances (and true points/shot for shooting!), but this post is already long enough.
